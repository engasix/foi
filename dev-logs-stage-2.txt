- So following is the complete protocl:
  1 - Item 5.D of Form ADV Part 1A explicitly asks the firm to break down its client base.
    - Target Field: Item 5.D.(1)(i) or Item 5.D.(2)(i)
    - Metric: High-Net-Worth Individuals
    - Logic: Look for firms where "High-Net-Worth Individuals" account for 80% to 100% of their total client count or regulatory assets under management (RAUM). Standard commercial RIAs service retail clients, while MFOs focus almost exclusively on ultra-high-net-worth (UHNW) individual brackets.
  2 - Evaluate Client Density (Item 5.F)
    - Family offices typically manage massive capital pooled across a very small number of families.
    - Target Fields: Item 5.F.(2)(a) (Total Number of Accounts) divided by Item 5.F.(2)(c) (Total Regulatory Assets Under Management).
    - Logic: Filter for a high AUM-to-Account ratio. If an entity manages $500 million across fewer than 10 or 20 total accounts, it is highly likely operating as a private multi-family office or a pseudo-SFO rather than a consumer wealth management firm.
  3 - Scan for Private Fund Affiliations (Schedule D, Section 7.B)
    - Family offices frequently set up private Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs), limited partnerships, or private equity pools to deploy family capital into deals.
    - Target Field: Schedule D, Section 7.B.(1) (Private Fund Reporting).
    - Logic: Look for firms managing private funds where the "Type of Private Fund" is listed as a Venture Capital Fund, Private Equity Fund, or Hedge Fund, but the fund's investors are listed in Section 7.B.(1).A.14 as primarily "High-Net-Worth Individuals" or "Individuals." This indicates a private family vehicle investing in alternatives.
  4 - Extract Owner and Executive Roles (Schedule A & B)
    - Family offices are usually controlled directly by the family members or a single holding company.
    - Target Field: Schedule A (Direct Owners and Executive Officers) and Schedule B (Indirect Owners).
    - Logic: Write script strings to search the names listed in these schedules against public lists of ultra-wealthy families or corporate executives. Additionally, isolate entities where the top owners are listed as a "Trust" or a single "Holding Company" rather than an outside private equity sponsor or corporate parent.
